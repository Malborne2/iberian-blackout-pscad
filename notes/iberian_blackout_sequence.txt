Iberian Blackout – 28 April 2025 – Sequence of EventsENTSO-E Final Report (20 March 2026)
Introduction and Scope
This document provides a detailed and summarised reconstruction of the cascading se-
quence of events that led to the total collapse of the Iberian Peninsula power system
on 28 April 2025, beginning at approximately 12:32:00 CEST and ending with complete
system separation at approximately 12:33:24 CEST—a collapse spanning less than 90
seconds.
The purpose of this document is to support the recreation of the event in DIgSI-
LENT PowerFactory, including correct protection relay settings, reactive power bal-
ance changes, voltage thresholds, and frequency trajectories.
All page and figure references correspond to the ENTSO-E ICS Investigation Expert
Panel Final Report, published 20 March 2026 (hereafter “the Report”).
Key Quantities at the Start of the Incident (12:32:00 CEST)
The following initial conditions apply to the Iberian system at 12:32:00 CEST (Report:
Chapter 2, pp. 29–115; Management Summary pp. 9–12):
 Frequency: 50.0 Hz (stable, minor fluctuations)
 400 kV voltage: Below 420 kV at all pilot nodes in Spain; within 380 kV to 420 kV
in Portugal
 Spain net exports: ≈5 GW (to France via AC lines and HVDC)
 HVDC Santa Llogaia–Baixas: 2×500 MW Spain→France; AC voltage setpoint
405 kV (Spain side), 395 kV (France side); absorbing 2×440 Mvar reactive on French
side, injecting 7.7 Mvar on Spanish side
 Spanish total generation: ≈27.2 GW; predominantly solar PV (≈18 GW), wind
(≈3.5 GW), with nuclear and gas as synchronous backstop
 Renewable generation mode: Fixed power factor (not voltage-control mode) —
critical context
 Two oscillatory episodes had occurred in the 30 minutes prior:
– 12:03–12:08: Converter-driven forced oscillation at 0.63 Hz, local to Iberian
Peninsula
– 12:19–12:22: Inter-area oscillation at 0.2 Hz (East–Centre–West CE mode)
 Several shunt reactors had been disconnected to address low voltages during oscil-
lation episodes up to 12:24.
Source: Report pp. 9–12 (Management Summary), Figures 1-2 and 1-3 (voltage pilot
nodes), Figure 1-4 (generation map), Figure 1-5 (oscillatory episodes), Section 3.1.2.2
(HVDC p. 136).

Detailed Sequence of Events
Pre-Incident Context: 09:00–12:32:00 CEST
Source: Report Chapter 2, pp. 29–115; Management Summary pp. 9–12.
From approximately 09:00 CEST, the voltage variability in Spain began to increase,
driven by high renewable generation (solar PV ≈18 GW, wind ≈3.5 GW). At approxi-
mately 10:30 CEST, the voltage in part of the 400 kV network briefly approached—but
did not exceed—435 kV (the Spanish upper operational limit) at the Olmedilla node.
Two oscillatory episodes occurred in the preceding 30 minutes:
1. 12:03–12:08 CEST: Converter-driven forced oscillation at 0.63 Hz, primarily
in the Spanish and Portuguese systems, with highest amplitude near Carmona–
Almaraz.
Triggered by an inverter-based power plant connected at the node of
highest amplitude.
2. 12:19–12:22 CEST: Inter-area oscillation at 0.2 Hz, East–Centre–West continen-
tal mode. Operators applied mitigation: reduced Spain–France exports, coupled
internal lines in southern Spain, changed HVDC operating mode.
The mitigation measures for the oscillations increased voltages across the Iberian system
as a side effect (reduced exports ⇒less reactive power exported ⇒higher local voltages).
At 12:32:00 CEST, the 400 kV voltage was below 420 kV at all buses; frequency was
nominally 50.0 Hz; no notable oscillation with amplitude above 20 mHz was detected.
Event Cluster 1 – 12:32:00 to 12:32:57: Pre-collapse Generation and Load
Changes (ES)
Source: Report Table 3-1 (p. 117), Section 3.1.1 (p. 122), Section 4.3 (p. 278).
Event 1 – Net Load Increase in Spanish Distribution Grids
 Time: 12:32:00–12:32:57 CEST
 Type: Net load increase (distribution level)
 Power Imbalance: +317.3 MW
 Reactive Power Change: Unknown/unquantified
 Voltage at onset: ≈412 kV (Carmona PMU, Figure 3-5, p. 127)
 Cause: Combination of: (a) disconnection of small embedded PV generators (<1 MW,
rooftop) triggered by overvoltage inverter protection; (b) actual increase in voltage-
dependent load due to rising voltage
 Protection setting: Small PV inverter overvoltage protection thresholds not stan-
dardised; two PV inverter manufacturers’ data showed correlation with 400 kV volt-
age rise (Figure 1-13, p. 20)
 PowerFactory note: Model as a step load increase of +317 MW at t = 0 s dis-
tributed across the Spanish network. This can be represented as a load increase
event or a partial disconnection of small PV units.

Model Topology Recommendations
Based on the Report, the following topology elements are critical for simulation fidelity:
 France–Spain interconnections: Three AC lines: Baixas (RTE)–Vic (RE) 400 kV,
Argia (RTE)–Arkale (RE) 220 kV, Argia (RTE)–Hernani (RE) 400 kV; plus HVDC
Santa Llogaia–Baixas (2×500 MW).
 Spain–Morocco interconnections: Two 400 kV AC lines (Puerto de la Cruz–
Beni Harchane; Puerto de la Cruz–Melloussa).
 Key generation substations: 400 kV TS 1–Granada (355 MW PV/wind/thermal),
400 kV TS 1/TS 2–Badajoz (727 MW PV/thermal), 400 kV TS 1–Seville (550 MW
PV), 400 kV TS 1–Segovia (117 MW wind), 400 kV TS 1–Cuenca (530 MW PV),
400 kV TS 1–M´alaga (154 MW PV).
 Synchronous generation: Nuclear plants (total ≈3,390 MW in Spain), combined-
cycle gas (≈719 MW tripped), pump-storage (≈3,031 MW in pumping mode before
incident).
Key Generator Modelling Parameters
 All RES generators: Fixed power factor mode (NOT voltage control mode). This
is the fundamental condition that prevented voltage damping.
 Synchronous generators (PO 7.4): Voltage control (AVR) active, but Q output
was not following Q-reference in many cases.
 Shunt reactors: Manually switched; do NOT model as automatic voltage regula-
tors. Model as manually connected/disconnected reactive compensation elements.
Note: several shunt reactors had been disconnected before 12:32:00 due to low volt-
ages during oscillations.
Protection Relay Implementation
1. Event 3 (Granada):
Implement OV relay on 220 kV transformer secondary:
threshold = 110%×220 kV = 242 kV, t = 0 s (instantaneous). This is the trigger for
the entire cascade.
2. Event 4b (Badajoz 2): OV relay Stage 1: 435.6 kV, t = 1.5 s. Note: actual
operating mechanism involved relay hysteresis (reset at 95% of threshold).
3. France–Spain OST protection (Events 31a/31b): Implement DRS loss-of-
synchronism protection. Setting: 2 complete voltage beats (power swings).
The relay must detect 10 decreasing then 4 increasing voltage semicycles to count
one beat; it trips after 2 such beats.
4. Morocco UF protection (Events 16a/16b): Underfrequency relay at 49.5 Hz
on Moroccan side with direct transfer trip to Spanish end.
5. Spanish LFDD (Section 3.10): Automatic load shedding at 49.0, 48.8, 48.6,
48.4, 48.2, 48.0 Hz; pump-storage at 49.5 and 49.3 Hz.

Summary: Root Causes and Contributing Factors
Source: Report Section 4.6 (Root Cause Tree, p. 331), Section 9.1 (pp. 451–454).
The Expert Panel identified the following root causes and contributing factors:
1. Root Cause 1 – Inadequate voltage control capability: RES generators op-
erating in fixed power factor mode did not provide reactive power control. Reactive
power absorption was insufficient and manually operated (shunt reactors).
2. Root Cause 2 – Protection relay settings non-compliant: Multiple gener-
ation facilities had overvoltage protection set below the voltage levels they were
required to withstand under applicable regulations (Order TED 749/2020 / EU
RfG).
3. Root Cause 3 – Converter-driven oscillations: The 0.63 Hz forced oscillation
(converter-driven) weakened the system and, combined with mitigating measures,
increased voltages.
4. Root Cause 4 – Narrow operating margin: Spain’s 400 kV operational ceiling
(435 kV) left only 5 kV before generator protection thresholds were reached.
5. Contributing factor – HVDC constant power mode: After AC line trips, the
HVDC continued to export power from the already-deficit Iberian system, worsening
the imbalance.
6. Contributing factor – Defence plan shortfall: LFDD achieved only 28.7% of
load shed vs. 45% target.
